# Business overview - findings

Generated by `src/02_business_overview.py` from `sql/01-03`. Monthly numbers use full months
only (2012-04 to 2015-02); the first and last month in the data are partial.
The data is synthetic (Maven Fuzzy Factory, built by Maven Analytics for teaching).

## (a) Trend in sessions and orders  - chart 01
- Monthly sessions grew from 3,734 (2012-04) to 23,778 (2015-02), about 6.4x.
- Monthly orders grew faster: from 99 to 2,067, about 21x. Orders outgrew traffic because conversion also improved (see b).
- Traffic is seasonal: it spikes every November-December. The busiest month was 2014-12 with 29,722 sessions and 2,314 orders.

## (b) Session-to-order conversion rate  - chart 02
- Conversion rose from 2.65% (2012-04) to 8.69% (2015-02).
- The clearest step up is around January 2013: 5.02% in 2012-12, 6.11% in 2013-01 and 6.93% in 2013-02.
- Three things changed in that same fortnight: /billing-2 went to all visitors (after 2013-01-05), a second product launched (2013-01-06) and /lander-2 started (2013-01-14). A trend line cannot separate these causes. That is exactly why the billing page needs a proper A/B test.

## (c) Most successful channels  - chart 03
- By size: paid_nonbrand dominates with 71.4% of sessions and 69.6% of revenue ($1,349,978).
- By quality: paid_brand converts best (7.79%, $4.72 revenue per session), followed by organic_search (7.51%, $4.53). These are visitors who already know the brand or found the site themselves.
- paid_social is the weakest channel: 3.21% conversion and $2.08 per session, about half of any other channel.

## (d) Revenue per order and per session  - chart 04
- Revenue per order was exactly $49.99 until the end of 2012, because the store sold a single product at one price.
- It rose to $54.16 by 2013-11 after product #2 launched, and to about $64.67 from 2014-02 once products #3 and #4 were on sale. It ended at $62.47 (2015-02).
- Revenue per session grew from $1.33 to $5.43, about 4.1x. It is conversion rate x revenue per order, so both improvements feed into it.

## Funnel and where the A/B test sits  - chart 05
- Of all sessions, 55.2% reach /products, 20.1% reach the cart, 13.6% reach shipping, 11.0% reach a billing page and 6.8% place an order.
- The billing page is the last step before an order. Over the whole period 62.1% of sessions that reached a billing page ordered (32,313 of 52,058), so more than a third of visitors who got as far as entering payment details still left.
- The A/B test in this project sits on exactly this step (billing -> order). Visitors there are the closest to buying, so a better billing page turns into orders directly.
